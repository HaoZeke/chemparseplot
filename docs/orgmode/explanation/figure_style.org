* Overview

The journal style has no hand-set sizes.
A preset records what a journal states: its column widths, the font sizes of its LaTeX class and its smallest lettering.
It also records the journal's minimum line weight, its raster resolution and the faces it names.
=figure_style()= derives every other length from those numbers and from the drafting standards of the International Organization for Standardization (ISO).
The colours come from the Ruhi palette (Ruhila Goswami).
They are checked under simulated colour-vision deficiency with the colour-difference formula of the International Commission on Illumination (CIE).
This page follows each number back to its source.

* Sizes from the journal

| Preset     | Journals                                                                                                | Publisher                                      |
|------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------------|
| =acs=      | Journal of Chemical Theory and Computation (JCTC), Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling (JCIM) | American Chemical Society (ACS)                |
| =aip=      | The Journal of Chemical Physics                                                                         | American Institute of Physics (AIP) Publishing |
| =elsevier= | Computer Physics Communications                                                                         | Elsevier                                       |
| =rsc=      | Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics                                                                     | Royal Society of Chemistry (RSC)               |

Column widths and page heights come from each publisher's author guidelines.
A guideline may give a length in two units, such as 240 pt and 3.33 in for an ACS column.
The two are then rounded conversions of each other.
The preset takes the smaller, so the figure fits both.

Text sizes are the =\normalsize=, =\small= and =\footnotesize= of each journal's LaTeX class in its two-column layout:

| Preset     | Class and layout              | Sizes (TeX pt) |
|------------+-------------------------------+----------------|
| =aip=      | revtex4-2, =reprint=          | 10, 9, 8       |
| =elsevier= | elsarticle, =5p=              | 10, 9, 8       |
| =rsc=      | RSC article template          | 9, 8, 7        |

The achemso class sets JCTC and JCIM only in a 12 pt submission layout.
The ACS sizes are therefore those of the published page, 10, 9 and 8 PostScript points.
TeX points (72.27 per inch) are converted to PostScript points (72 per inch).
No size goes below the journal's smallest lettering.
The x and y labels use the body size, and tick labels and legends =\small=.

* Panel shape

A panel takes its shape from the data where they give one, as an energy landscape does with equal x and y scales.
Otherwise it is half again as wide as it is tall.
Tufte advises graphics "about 50 percent wider than tall" when the data suggest no shape (1983, p. 190).

* Lettering and lines

Text is set in the theme's face, Atkinson Hyperlegible.
The Braille Institute released it in 2019 for readers with low vision.
It gives easily confused characters such as I, l and 1 distinct shapes.
The ACS, Elsevier and RSC guidelines name Helvetica or Arial.
Those faces replace Atkinson Hyperlegible where it is not installed.

ISO 3098-1:2015 gives the proportions of lettering as fractions of the capital height h.
With h measured from the outline of "H" in the chosen face at the body size, the style sets:

| Length                      | ISO 3098-1 type B | Used for                      |
|-----------------------------+-------------------+-------------------------------|
| letter stroke d             | 0.1 h             | base of the line widths       |
| x-height                    | 0.7 h             | major tick length             |
| descender                   | 0.3 h             | minor tick length             |
| word space                  | 0.6 h             | tick-to-label and label pads  |
| character space             | 0.2 h             | gaps around panels and labels |
| baseline pitch (mixed case) | 1.5 h             | spacing of stacked labels     |

Markers are as tall as a capital.
ISO 128-2:2020 steps line widths by a factor of the square root of two.
It pairs them as narrow, wide and extra-wide in the ratio 1 : 2 : 4.
The narrow width is one step below the letter stroke, d/sqrt(2), so spines and ticks are lighter than the text.
It never goes below the journal's minimum line weight.
Data lines are wide.
In Atkinson Hyperlegible at the ACS body size, h is 6.68 pt and d/sqrt(2) is 0.47 pt, below the ACS minimum of 0.5 pt.
The minimum therefore binds: spines and ticks are 0.5 pt, data lines 1 pt and major ticks 4.7 pt long.

Dashed series use the ISO 128-2 line types 02 (dashed), 07 (dotted), 10 (dashed dotted) and 04 (long-dashed dotted).
Their dashes, gaps and dots are 12, 3 and 0.5 line widths long, and long dashes 24.
Matplotlib scales dash patterns by the line width, so the patterns stay in proportion at any width.

* Numbers

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) advises choosing prefixes so that numerical values lie between 0.1 and 1000.
That advice is in section 7.9 of its Special Publication 811.
Tick labels keep that range in plain numbers and switch to powers of ten, written with mathtext, outside it.
They never print an offset.
Errors that span decades go on log scales, with decades labelled as powers of ten.
A relative error below machine epsilon cannot be resolved and would vanish from a log scale, so it is drawn at epsilon.

* Colour

** The Ruhi palette

| Colour   | sRGB      | Contrast on white |
|----------+-----------+-------------------|
| teal     | =#004D40= |               9.8 |
| magenta  | =#D81B60= |               4.9 |
| sky      | =#1E88E5= |               3.7 |
| coral    | =#FF655D= |               2.9 |
| sunshine | =#F1DB4B= |               1.4 |

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 ask for a contrast of 3:1 for graphical objects (criterion 1.4.11).
For text they ask for 4.5:1 (criterion 1.4.3).
Teal, sky and magenta pass the first on white; coral and sunshine do not.
=series_style= therefore colours lines with teal, sky and magenta only.
The default colour cycle puts sunshine and coral last.
Direct labels take their line's colour.
Where needed it is darkened at the same hue until it reaches 4.5:1 after rounding to 8-bit values.

** Colour-vision deficiency

Colours are compared with CIEDE2000 (ISO/CIE 11664-6) in CIELAB.
Protan, deutan and tritan vision are simulated by the full-severity matrices of Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009).
Each palette's most confusable pair is the one with the smallest difference:

| Vision | Ruhi | Okabe-Ito | Closest Ruhi pair |
|--------+------+-----------+-------------------|
| normal | 19.8 |      21.7 | coral, magenta    |
| protan | 11.4 |      12.2 | teal, magenta     |
| deutan | 16.0 |      11.6 | coral, magenta    |
| tritan |  9.5 |      10.9 | coral, magenta    |

The Ruhi palette keeps its colours further apart than the Okabe-Ito palette for deutan vision.
It keeps them less far apart for normal, protan and tritan vision.
Colour alone is therefore not enough to tell series apart.
=series_style= pairs each colour with an ISO 128-2 line type, and lines are labelled directly where there is room.
The colours that pass 3:1 come first, the highest contrast leading.
Each next colour is the one farthest from those already chosen, by the smallest difference over all four vision types.
The colours that fail follow in the same way.
The order is teal, sky, magenta, sunshine, coral.

** Colour maps

=ruhi_seq= runs from a dark wine (teal's lightness and chroma at magenta's hue) through magenta and coral to sunshine.
Chroma and hue follow monotone cubic curves in the CIELAB lightness L*.
The table is spaced evenly in CIEDE2000 arc length (Kovesi, 2015).
Teal and sky are left out.
Sky's lightness lies between magenta's and coral's.
A map through sky therefore turns the hue by 94 and then 116 degrees over 15.5 units of L*.
Its lightness is then no longer monotone for protan, deutan or tritan vision.
A map that starts at teal itself turns the hue by 166 degrees on the way to magenta.
For protan vision its lightness is then not monotone either.
=ruhi_div= runs from teal through sky to a neutral grey at sunshine's lightness.
From there it runs through coral and magenta to the dark wine.
Both arms share one lightness schedule (Moreland, 2009).
An entry on one arm has the same L* as its mirror image on the other.

=colormap_report= measures both, with viridis for comparison:

| Map        | L* monotone, all four vision types | Step uniformity, normal vision |
|------------+------------------------------------+--------------------------------|
| =ruhi_seq= | yes                                |                          0.003 |
| =ruhi_div= | each arm                           |                          0.159 |
| =viridis=  | yes                                |                          0.149 |

Step uniformity is the coefficient of variation of the CIEDE2000 steps between neighbouring entries.
A value of 0 means every step looks the same size.
=ruhi_seq= is the default for surfaces and landscapes.
=ruhi_div= is for signed quantities centred on zero.
Its steps are less even than those of =ruhi_seq=.
Lines coloured from a map use only the entries with a 3:1 contrast on the background (=contrast_limited=).
The earlier bands of a nudged elastic band run are such lines.

* Labels and legends

Direct labels sit at the end of each line, one character space plus half a marker away.
Labels closer than one baseline pitch are moved apart as little as possible in the least-squares sense.
Subtracting k pitches from the k-th label turns the spacing constraint into an ordering constraint.
The pool-adjacent-violators algorithm solves that exactly.
Constrained layout then makes room for the labels and moves the line ends.
The labels are spread again until the ends move by less than one output pixel.

A legend is placed inside the panel at the first of matplotlib's locations that is clear.
A location is clear when the legend's box touches no line, marker, filled area, image or text.
When every location covers data, the legend goes in one row above the panel.

* Files

=save_figure= keeps the figure's exact size, so the width stays the column width.
Fonts are embedded, as The Journal of Chemical Physics asks of every figure.
They are embedded as TrueType (=pdf.fonttype= 42) rather than matplotlib's default Type 3.
PDF and SVG files carry no creation date, and SVG ids come from a fixed salt.
The same script therefore writes the same bytes on every run.
